# Name

## Relay

**Carry the right few things through each part of your day.**

## The big idea

Relay turns Dandelion's Wake, Morning, Afternoon, and Evening blocks into a sequence of calm handoffs. The home screen opens to **Now**: the current block, its small stack of actions, and one obvious next action—not a miniature project-management dashboard. Nathan can complete with one tap, move with one tap, or open a bottom sheet for any date, notes, links, editing, Schedule & Block, or Complete & Next; every change offers undo. At each block boundary, Relay asks for a ten-second handoff: finish what remains, deliberately carry it forward, or leave it for later. Waiting items surface as a compact “Waiting on” pulse at useful moments. Dandelion remains the source of truth; Relay makes its deliberately broad time blocks feel alive on the phone.

## A day with it

Nathan wakes to a quiet notification: “Wake · 2 things.” He taps it, sees the two actions, checks one off, and moves the other to Evening with a single tap; an Undo bar appears. In the morning, Relay opens directly to Morning, while a slim overdue strip keeps yesterday visible without taking over the screen. Walking into a church meeting, he opens its project and taps the persistent capture button: “Ask Maria for the room key, tomorrow morning.” The project is already filled in, and he saves without breaking the conversation.

At the end of work, Relay gives one grouped nudge: “Afternoon handoff · 3 remain.” From the notification he completes one; in the app he sends one to tomorrow and uses Complete & Next on the last. Evening shows only what belongs to Evening. Before putting the phone down, a small Waiting pulse reminds him that a reply has been sitting for several days, so he adds a follow-up action. The day feels closed rather than merely abandoned.

## Why Nathan will love it

- **The current block is instantly usable.** Today and overdue items remain first-class, but Wake, Morning, Afternoon, and Evening become focused working views instead of headings in a long page.
- **Every common action is fast and forgiving.** Complete, cancel, tomorrow, another time block, and the next few days are immediate; Undo replaces confirmation. Any future date stays one sheet away.
- **Capture respects context.** A universal capture button asks only what and when, inherits an open project, and leaves notes, links, type, and assignee easy to add a minute later.
- **Reminders help close loops, not create noise.** Start prompts and end-of-block handoffs are grouped, configurable by weekday and project/action class, and silent when nothing needs attention.
- **Nothing useful is stranded.** Projects, action details, notes, links, full editing, blocking actions, Complete & Next, and a dedicated Waiting view all remain accessible without crowding Now. The same native shell can later open into the student's familiar School, Store, and Bank mode and stay signed in.

## The bet

**A task app should organize the moment of transition, not just the list.** Relay bets that one current-block screen plus a deliberate end-of-block handoff will make Nathan return to Dandelion throughout the day—and end more days knowing exactly what was finished, moved, or still waiting.
