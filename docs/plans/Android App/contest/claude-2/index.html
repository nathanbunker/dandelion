Seedclock

Your day lives on your lock screen, one part at a time.

Most to-do apps wait to be opened. Seedclock comes to you as one living notification, the Now card. It shows the part of the day you're in, Wake, Morning, Afternoon or Evening, with a ✔ beside each action. When the part of the day changes, the card rolls over. Reminders aren't a pile of pings. They're the same card speaking up, once, at the right moment.

A dandelion seed head is called a "clock": children blow on it to tell the time. Dandelion already splits the day into four parts. Seedclock tells that time.

1 notification, never a pile 0 taps to see what's next ≤5 alerts on a weekday, by default Native Kotlin + Compose
The idea

One card. It changes; it doesn't multiply.

There's only ever one Seedclock notification while the day is going on. Everything that other apps do with new alerts, Seedclock does by changing that card.

The app

And when you do open it, two taps does anything.

The app is the full-control companion: Today by time of day, other days, Waiting, Projects, and one action sheet that's the same everywhere. Every change is immediate, with Undo and no "are you sure?".

Reminders

Helpful at the edges of the day. Quiet in between.

Dandelion says what's in each part of the day. The phone says when the parts start and end, with three profiles (Weekdays, Saturday, Sunday) you can reassign. Each moment can be Alert, Card only or Off, per part of the day and per profile.

≤ 5

alerts on an ordinary weekday with the defaults. Just 2 (Morning and Evening start) on a day you keep up.

Rules for kinds of actions

First match wins. No per-action settings.

  • might / would like to / goal → Quiet: on the card, never alerts
  • waiting → only in the Waiting check
  • Garden project on Sundays → Hidden from the card
  • everything else → Nudge

Never naggy: one alert per moment, never repeated · no alert if nothing's left · Snooze 30m re-alerts once · Quiet today · swipe the card away to hide it until the next part of the day.

MomentWhat the card doesDefault
Start of a part of the dayRolls over, with a message you write ("Time to be done with work.")Alert at Morning and Evening; silent otherwise
End of a part of the day"Morning ended · 2 left"; leftovers stay with ✔ and →Alert at the end of Morning, Afternoon, Evening, only if Nudge items remain
Waiting checkAdds the oldest waiting items (7+ days) with Follow upMon and Thu, start of Evening
Day doneSummary, then clears itselfSilent

Under the hood: the card and the next wake-ups come from one pure Kotlin function, unit-tested with a fake clock. Boundaries use exact alarms (USE_EXACT_ALARM, fine for a sideloaded app); a 15-minute background refresh, plus a refresh after every change and at every boundary, keeps the card current.

Key facts

How it's built and how it gets on the S23

Minimum Android
Android 9 (API 28)
Target and compile API 36. The S23 gets everything; the Note8 runs student mode from the same APK.
Stack
Kotlin + Compose
Material 3, Retrofit, OkHttp, kotlinx.serialization, WorkManager, DataStore. No Hilt, no local database.
Where the code lives
android/ in this repo
A pure-Kotlin core module for the reminder logic, plus the app module.
Sign-in
Device tokens
Password or email link → pending → Nathan approves on a desktop Devices page. The token never expires; revoke anytime.
Server
Shared services
New /api/v1/personal/* endpoints over services that the /m/* pages switch to as well, with ownership checks and server-side undo receipts.
Two modes
One APK
workflowType picks the personal or student screens. Only personal mode runs the Now card.

Setup, once

  1. Set ANDROID_HOME and put platform-tools on PATH (an agent can do this).
  2. Point Gradle at Android Studio's bundled JDK 21 (one line in your user gradle.properties). Maven keeps Java 25.
  3. The Gradle wrapper is committed, so there's nothing to install.
  4. Create a release signing key outside the repo, and back it up.
  5. On the S23: Developer options → USB and Wireless debugging; lock-screen notifications set to Details; Seedclock in Never sleeping apps.

Getting it on the phone, and updating it

Plug in (or connect over wireless debugging) and run .\gradlew.bat installRelease. Updates are the same command; the same signing key keeps the sign-in and settings. The debug build installs alongside as Seedclock Dev and can point at your local server (localhost:8089 through adb reverse; 10.0.2.2 on the emulator), so development never touches the copy you rely on. No store needed.

Build order

Every step is usable. The risky part goes first.

0
Spike, about a day.

A throwaway Now card on the S23. Can ✔ run from the lock screen? Do five rows fit? Does it survive Samsung's battery management? You can: see the card on your own lock screen, and we decide on the fallback if needed.

1
Server: devices and reading.

Device sign-in, the Devices page, ownership checks, today's actions as JSON; /m/todo and /m/project switch to the shared service. You can: approve and revoke devices.

2
App: sign in and look.

Today by time of day, other days, Waiting, Projects, action detail, the offline strip. You can: stay signed in and see everything.

3
Act on actions.

Complete, cancel, move chips, pick a date, server-backed Undo, notes, links. You can: run your day from the app.

4
Capture and edit.

Quick capture with project context, Edit details, Schedule & Block, Complete & Next, waiting Follow up. You can: do everything /m/* does, plus waiting items.

5
The Now card.

✔, undo, leftovers, inline capture, pin a project, rollover, background refresh, a Quick Settings tile. You can: keep the day on your lock screen.

6
Reminders.

Profiles, rules, start and end alerts, waiting check, snooze, Quiet today, time travel for testing. You can: live with the full Seedclock.

7
Release.

A release-signed, production-only build. You can: retire the /m/* pages on your phone.

Then student mode: extract the School, Store and Bank services, add /api/v1/student/*, add a School · Store · Bank screen set, install on the Note8. Sign-in, devices, day navigation and Undo are reused unchanged.

Honest about the risk

The whole idea rests on Samsung's notification behavior, so step 0 tests it before anything else is built. If One UI asks for an unlock before a lock-screen ✔ runs, ticking still takes one fingerprint touch, with no app to open. If custom rows render badly, the fallback is a standard notification with three buttons (✔ first item, All to tomorrow, Open). Either way, steps 1 to 4 deliver a complete native app on their own. The card can lag a desktop edit by up to about 15 minutes; ↻ refreshes it.

Why this one

Every entry organizes the day into four parts. Seedclock moves it out of the app.

Sundial

Built around a satisfying end-of-pass ritual, a swipe-per-item Sweep, plus a widget. Great if Nathan wants a deliberate routine four times a day.

Relay

A calm, focused Now screen inside the app, with a handoff at each boundary. Great if the app's home screen is where Nathan wants to live.

Seedclock

The list comes to him. One notification is the to-do list, ✔ on every row, capture typed straight into it, and reminders that are the same card changing state. Checking off the current thing takes no navigation at all.
