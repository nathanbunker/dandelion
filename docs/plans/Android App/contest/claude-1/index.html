Sundial
Dandelion App Contest · entry claude-1

Every “when” is one thumb.

A native Android app for Nathan's personal Dandelion actions. The day comes in four passes (Wake, Morning, Afternoon, Evening). Check things off with a tap. Move anything, to any day and any time of day, with a single press-and-drag on the Dial, and see how full each day already is before you let go.

The idea

Moving is the hard part. Sundial makes it one gesture.

Checking something off is already one tap on today's mobile pages. Moving isn't. It takes a pop-up that stops at seven days, and the full edit form beyond that. Yet moving is what happens to nearly everything he doesn't finish, and moves more than a week out are common (P-9).

So Sundial gives "when" a single, consistent control: press and hold any action, slide around the ring for the day, slide inward for the time of day, lift. The same Dial picks the date when he moves an item, sweeps a pass, captures something new, or writes a follow-up.

Around the Dial

  • Today, shaped by passes: the current pass is open and the rest are folded.
  • Swipe right to complete, swipe left for one-tap moves, hold for the Dial.
  • The Sweep: one grouped reminder as a pass closes, then the leftovers card by card.
  • One-sentence capture, parsed by Dandelion's own sentence service, with a live preview.
  • A home-screen widget showing the current pass with checkboxes.
  • A Waiting tab, with stale items brought into the Evening Sweep.
Try it

Drag the Dial

Hold and drag on the ringToday is Thursday, Nov 12

  1. Around the ring: one notch per day, today at the top. One full turn is 28 days; keep going for the next lap.
  2. Into the center: pick Wake, Morning, Afternoon or Evening. Lift on the ring to keep the current time of day.
  3. The marks on each notch show how many actions are already on that day.
  4. Back out by returning to the very center, or by dragging off the Dial.

On the phone, each day gives a light haptic tick and each week a stronger one. A tap mode with Tomorrow · +1 wk · +2 wk · +1 mo · 📅 chips is always there too, so the gesture is never the only way to pick a date. The density marks in this demo are sample data.

Reminders

Helpful, grouped, and quiet when there's nothing to say

Two kinds, per pass

  • Pass start (P-22): "Evening · 3 things…" with Open, Snooze 30m and Quiet today.
  • Sweep (P-21): "Morning closes · 3 left", 15 minutes before the pass ends, with Sweep, All to next pass and Snooze 30m.
  • Never per action. Nothing fires for an empty pass. A new reminder replaces the last one.
  • Watches (P-23): named rules by project and action type, on a pass × weekday grid. The default watch is everything except might and would like to.
  • Pause for vacations. Start and Sweep reminders are separate Android notification channels.

Key facts

How it's built, and how it gets on the phone

The app

  • Minimum Android 9 (API 28), target and compile 36
  • Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Material 3
  • Retrofit, OkHttp, kotlinx.serialization
  • Glance widget, AlarmManager, WorkManager, DataStore
  • Lives in android/ in this repo
  • dev and prod flavors, installed side by side

Sign-in and devices

  • Password or email link, then pending until approved on a new Settings → Devices page
  • A per-device key on the existing WebApiClient, stored only as a hash
  • Never expires; Revoke ends it
  • Every action endpoint checks it's his own personal action (G-8)

Server

  • New services in org.dandeliondaily.mobile.service, used by both the /m/* pages and /api/v1/app/* (G-13)
  • Server-side undo tokens that also restore unblocked actions
  • One migration: device columns and an action_undo table

Build, test, install (agent commands)

Getting it on the S23 Ultra

  • Once, by hand: turn on Developer options, USB debugging and Wireless debugging. Accept the USB prompt, and pair over Wi-Fi with a code.
  • Every install and update: an agent runs tools\phone.ps1 install-prod. The same signing key keeps the sign-in, settings and widget.
  • Local server: the emulator uses 10.0.2.2:8089; the phone uses adb reverse tcp:8089 tcp:8089.
  • No store listing (G-11, G-14).
Build order

Every step is usable

  1. SkeletonThe Android project, flavors and scripts. "Sundial Dev" installs on the S23 from a command.
  2. DevicesSign in, approve on the desktop, stay signed in, revoke.
  3. Read the dayToday, other days, Projects, Waiting, detail. The /m/* pages switch to the same services.
  4. Act and undoComplete, cancel, move tray, undo. The daily driver starts here.
  5. The DialGesture plus tap mode and density marks. If the gesture doesn't win after a week, tap mode and chips carry P-9 on their own.
  6. Capture and editCapture with preview, project context, tile, share target; edit, notes, Block on, Complete & next.
  7. Reminders and the SweepPass schedule, watches, notifications with actions.
  8. WidgetThe current pass on the home screen.
  9. ReplaceTwo weeks alongside the web pages, then stop using /m/* on the phone.

Student mode next: the same APK and sign-in, with a School · Store · Bank screen set selected by workflowType. Android 9 is already the minimum, so it installs on the Note8 as is, and stays signed in.

Honest risks

What could go wrong

  • The Dial gesture might need tuning, or might not suit him. It's built and judged on its own in step 4, with tap mode as the fallback.
  • Samsung battery management can delay alarms. There's a setup check and a 3-day field test.
  • Undo is subtle when a completion unblocks other actions. One service handles it, with a full snapshot and a "changed elsewhere" guard.
  • Java 25 on the PC is newer than Gradle supports well. The build uses Android Studio's bundled JDK 21.
Why this one

Common ground, plus one sharp idea

All three entries close each part of the day with a short review of what's left. That's common ground, and the funded app will likely have it. Seedclock bets on a living lock-screen notification. Relay bets on a calm Now screen and deliberate handoffs. Sundial bets that moving is where the current pages are clunkiest, and makes every "when" in the app one gesture with the week's load in view. Its plan maps every requirement to the existing code, and its signature risk has a built-in fallback.

Every claim on this page is planned in proposal.md.
