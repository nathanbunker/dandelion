Sundial · claude-1

Kotlin + Compose · minSdk 28 · exact alarms · Glance widget
Pitch pagepitch.mdproposal.md
The bet: moving an item, not checking it off, is where the current pages are clunkiest. The Dial makes "when" a single press-and-drag: around the ring picks the day (28 days per lap, with haptic ticks), inward picks the time of day, and each day shows how full it already is. The same Dial is used everywhere a date is chosen. Changed after the pitches: its pitch was built on the Sweep, but all three entries had arrived at that independently, so it moved its signature to the Dial and said so openly.

Strengths

  • It answers your requirement P-9 directly: moves of more than a week are common, and here they're one gesture with the day's load in view. A tap mode, jump chips (+1 week, +2 weeks, +1 month) and a date picker mean the gesture is never the only way.
  • The most complete reminder design: a start and an end "Sweep" for each part of the day, named "watches" (filters by project and type, in a part-of-day × weekday grid), separate notification channels, Snooze, Quiet today, and Pause for vacations.
  • The most ways to capture: the + button, project screens, the widget, a Quick Settings tile, a launcher shortcut and the Android share sheet (share a web page and it becomes an action with its link). A live preview shows how the sentence was understood.
  • The best developer experience: env.ps1 and phone.ps1 scripts, dev and prod builds installable side by side, a debug broadcast that fires any reminder, and the version number taken from the git commit count.
  • Its references to the code checked out: WebApiClient, startsWithProjectToken, ActionChangeLogService, ProjectActionBlockerManager.

Concerns

  • It relies on hidden gestures: swipe right completes, swipe left opens the move chips, press-and-hold opens the Dial. They're quick once learned, but you can't see them, and you said the current interface is "clunky", not slow.
  • The Dial's 28-day lap needs fine thumb control. The proposal admits it may need tuning and plans a fallback, which is good, but it's the least proven idea among the three entries.
  • The largest scope: Glance widget, Quick Settings tile, share target, a new endpoint for the per-day load marks, and the Dial component itself. The build order puts these late, which helps.
  • It proposes a new ActionQuickChangeService without noticing that ActionCompletionService already handles completion for the desktop dashboard and MCP. That's a small risk of two completion paths drifting apart (G-13).
Love it8.5The Dial and the Sweep fit how you work. Points off for gestures you have to learn. Requirements9.0Every ID is mapped, including email-link sign-in, with clear and open notes. Buildable7.5Grounded and well ordered, but the most surface area, and it duplicates completion logic. Workflow9.0The most concrete scripts and the dev/prod setup. Stands out8.5The Dial is unique and memorable. Pitch and page9.0An interactive Dial demo, a full set of mock screens, and an honest risk section.

Seedclock · claude-2

Kotlin + Compose · minSdk 28 · exact alarms · ongoing notification
Pitch pagepitch.mdproposal.md
The bet: the main screen is the notification, not the app. One ongoing Now card shows the current part of the day with a ✔ on each row, rolls over at each boundary, and its own state changes are the reminders. Unfinished items stay on it as leftovers. Changed after the pitches: it dropped its own Sweep because it overlapped Sundial's, and handles leftovers on the card instead. It added inline capture on the card and "pin a project to the card".

Strengths

  • Checking off takes no navigation: from the lock screen or the notification shade, over any app. That matches the quick-check-off page you described for answer 8.
  • Reminders that can't pile up: one card, one alert per moment, never repeated, and "≤5 alerts on a weekday by default". The rules classify actions as Nudge, Quiet or Hidden by project and type, with weekday profiles. This fits your "groups and classes, not individual actions".
  • Visible tap targets throughout the app, with no hidden gestures. One action sheet everywhere, with move chips reaching Next week and +2 wks and a calendar with time-of-day chips.
  • A thoughtful waiting follow-up: Follow up creates a blocking action (Schedule & Block), so the waiting item comes back by itself once the follow-up is done.
  • Built to reduce risk: all the reminder logic is a pure-Kotlin buildCard()/planWakeups() module tested on the JVM, there's a time-travel broadcast, and step 0 is a one-day spike on your S23 to test the riskiest assumption before anything else is built. It's also the only Claude entry that accounts for linked-project twins (SharedActionSiblings) when completing and undoing.

Concerns

  • It's unproven whether Samsung runs a ✔ on the lock screen without unlocking, and how it draws custom notification rows. The proposal says so and has a fallback, but the main idea depends on it.
  • An always-there card can become wallpaper you stop seeing. Leftovers and "Day done" are designed against this; it's still a real risk.
  • The card can be up to about 15 minutes behind changes made on the desktop. It refreshes on every change, every boundary and every app open, and push is listed as a later option.
  • Moves beyond tomorrow mean opening the app's action sheet. That's two taps away, but not on the card.
  • Like Sundial, it creates a new completion service next to the existing ActionCompletionService.
Love it9.0The closest match to the way you described wanting to use it, and its reminders are designed not to nag. Requirements9.0Every ID is mapped, and it notes openly where it only partly meets two of them. Buildable8.0The spike-first order and pure core module are strong; custom notifications are fiddly to build. Workflow9.0Clear setup, dev.ps1, and time travel for testing reminders. Stands out9.0The only entry whose main surface isn't an app screen. Pitch and page9.0Four card-state mocks, a tappable ✔, and a clear "honest about the risk" section.

Relay · openai

Kotlin + Compose · minSdk 28 · WorkManager (inexact) · app-first
Pitch pagepitch.mdproposal.md
The bet: a calm, app-first runway for the current part of the day: one large Now card, the rest visible On deck, and the same Done, Move and More buttons everywhere. There are no required gestures, no widget and no ongoing notification. Changed after the pitches: it dropped the end-of-block ritual as common ground and sharpened "one obvious next action".

Strengths

  • The strongest server engineering: idempotency keys on every change, version checks on edits, and a sign-in where the phone gets no credential until you approve it, plus CSRF protection, constant-time comparison and rate limits.
  • The best reading of the existing code. It's the only entry that noticed ActionCompletionService.closeActionWithHandoff already exists to reuse, and that ActionRecoveryService is session-based and can't power undo. Both checked out.
  • Validates the interaction first: build step 1 is a fake-data app on your phone, so you judge the runway before any server work. Before refactoring, it adds tests that capture the current /m/* behavior.
  • Calm and accessible: visible controls only, TalkBack, 200% font size and 48dp tap targets, with explicit when and time-of-day controls so capture never depends on the sentence being parsed correctly.
  • The smallest scope of the three, so the most likely to ship early.

Concerns

  • Reminders are deliberately inexact (WorkManager, no exact alarms). Samsung's battery management can push them well past the end of a part of the day, and late reminders are discarded after 45 minutes. That's the weakest answer to "alert me at appropriate times" (P-21, P-22).
  • The Now card highlights the first item in the server's order, which isn't a real plan for your day. The proposal names this risk itself.
  • Capture asks for what, when, time of day and project, with sentence parsing secondary. It's reliable, but slower than typing one sentence. It also defaults to your most recently used project, which could file an action in the wrong place.
  • It drops email-link sign-in. That's allowed (G-3 says password or email link), but it's less than the other two offer. The setup steps are the least concrete of the three (no helper scripts).
  • Its lock-screen mock shows action text, but the proposal's default hides that text until you unlock. It's a small mismatch.
Love it7.0Pleasant and clear, but the least delightful, and its reminders may come late. Requirements8.0Every ID is claimed. The reminder timing and the dropped email link are weaker. Buildable9.0The best server plan, the fake-data prototype first, and the smallest scope. Workflow7.5Correct, but less specific (no scripts, and a LAN-IP fallback). Stands out6.5The runway is a refinement of a familiar list app. Pitch and page8.0The most polished design, but four mocks and no interactivity.
Common ground

What all three agree on, worth treating as settled

Each entry worked out its own design, yet they agree on all of the following. Whichever one wins, these points are low-risk to adopt.

App and build

  • Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Material 3, one activity, Retrofit and OkHttp
  • App code in android/ in this repository, with a committed Gradle wrapper
  • minSdk 28 (Android 9), target 36: the same APK later runs on the Note8, and none of the three needs a newer minimum
  • No local database and no offline queue (G-9)
  • Run Gradle with Android Studio's bundled JDK, not the machine's Java 25
  • Install on the phone with adb install -r and a release key that's backed up; reach the local server with adb reverse tcp:8089 from the phone and 10.0.2.2 from the emulator

Server and behavior

  • Each device gets a credential on the existing WebApiClient table, stored as a hash, which you approve and revoke on a new desktop Devices page
  • New services shared by the /m/* pages and the app's new API (G-13), which also closes the ownership gap left open earlier
  • Undo runs on the server from a snapshot of what changed, including actions that were unblocked, and refuses if the action has changed since
  • The phone holds the reminder schedule and rules; Dandelion only knows the four parts of the day
  • Some way of handling what's left at the end of each part of the day
  • Waiting items shown oldest first, with Follow up and Got it

What I verified

Rules, facts and claims

Contest rules

  • All six contest commits only touched the contestant's own folder
  • No pitch.md was edited after phase 1 (each was committed once)
  • Every proposal covers sections 1–12, including "What changed" and "Why this one"
  • Every index.html is one self-contained file with no external fonts, scripts or requests, and includes the phone viewport tag
  • Each page's main risk caveat matches its proposal (Seedclock's lock-screen fallback, Sundial's Dial fallback, Relay's inexact timing)
  • Comparisons between entries are fair. Each one describes the others accurately from what was visible to it (the other entries' pitches).

Code claims

  • Classes and methods cited across entries exist: WebApiClient, ApiKeyAuthFilter, ApiRateLimitFilter (60 per minute), ActionChangeLogService, SharedActionSiblings, startsWithProjectToken, saveNewActionFromSentence, ActionNotesService.append, LinkedMeetingService.actionTypeError, ActionCompletionService.closeActionWithHandoff
  • The project: capture prefix exists in ActionSentenceImportService
  • The next migration would be v5.15.sql (the latest is v5.14.sql)
  • Relay is right that ActionRecoveryService stores its state in the HTTP session
  • Sundial and Seedclock both missed the existing ActionCompletionService, which the desktop dashboard and MCP already use for completion
  • I couldn't confirm how the pages look at phone width: headless Edge has a minimum window width, so all three looked cut off in the same way. Worth a look on your phone.
Your call

Questions that should decide this

My scores rest on guesses about your preferences. Your answers to these matter more than the weights.

  1. Where do you want to see "now" during the day?On the lock screen and in the notification shade (Seedclock), on a home-screen widget (Sundial), or inside the app (Relay)? Or more than one?
  2. Gestures or visible buttons?Swipe to complete and hold for the Dial (Sundial), or everything as visible taps (Seedclock, Relay)?
  3. How punctual do reminders need to be?"At 11:45, before Morning ends" (exact alarms: Sundial, Seedclock), or "sometime around the end of Morning" (Relay)?
  4. How do you like to capture?Type one sentence and let Dandelion work it out (Sundial, Seedclock), or set when and project explicitly (Relay)?
  5. At the end of a part of the day:a short deliberate Sweep (Sundial), leftovers that simply stay on the card (Seedclock), or a nudge that opens the app (Relay)?
  6. Combine, or pick one as is?My recommendation is Seedclock as the base, with the borrowings listed under "The verdict". Or would you rather fund one entry unchanged?
